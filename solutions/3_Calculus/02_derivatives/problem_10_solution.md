# Exercise 10 — The Tangent Line as an Application of the Derivative

## 1. Problem Statement
For $f(x)=x^2-3x+1$, find the tangent line at $x=2$. Give the point of tangency, derivative value, line equation and a point-substitution check.

## 2. Theoretical Background and Concepts
The derivative $f'(a)$ is the tangent slope at $(a,f(a))$. The point-slope equation for the tangent line is $y-f(a)=f'(a)(x-a)$.

## 3. Detailed Step-by-Step Solution
Evaluate the function at $x=2$:

$$
f(2)=2^2-3(2)+1=4-6+1=-1.
$$

The point of tangency is $(2,-1)$. Differentiate term by term:

$$
f'(x)=2x-3.
$$

Hence the slope at $x=2$ is

$$
f'(2)=2(2)-3=1.
$$

The tangent line follows from point-slope form:

$$
y-(-1)=1(x-2).
$$

Simplifying gives $y+1=x-2$, or

$$
y=x-3.
$$

## 4. Verification and Consistency Checks
At $(2,-1)$, the tangent equation gives $-1=2-3$, so the point lies on it. Its coefficient of $x$ is $1$, matching the derivative. A further consistency check is that the graph minus the tangent is

$$
f(x)-(x-3)=x^2-4x+4=(x-2)^2.
$$

This difference vanishes to second order at $x=2$, consistent with tangency rather than merely crossing.

## 5. Final Answer
Point $(2,-1)$, slope $f'(2)=1$, tangent line $y=x-3$.
